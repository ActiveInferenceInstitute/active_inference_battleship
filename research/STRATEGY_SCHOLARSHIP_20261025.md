### Statistical interpretation to retain

For each paired endpoint difference D, the frozen sign-test null is P(D > 0 | D is non-tied) = P(D < 0 | D is non-tied) = 1/2. Given n_non-tied independent signs, the positive count has the Binomial(n_non-tied, 1/2) reference distribution. Integer endpoints use exact zero ties; normalized-entropy differences within 10^-10 are classified as sign-test ties. With no non-tied signs, p = 1 is a declared convention and supplies no equivalence evidence. This is a statement about the *direction* of a non-tied difference, not equality of means. For example, a difference of -1 with probability 0.9 and +9 with probability 0.1 has mean zero but fails sign balance; a difference of -100 or +1 with equal probability has sign balance but a nonzero mean. These examples are algebraic illustrations, not study results. The historical source's tie allocation and NIST's tie exclusion must not be blended. [Dixon and Mood, pp. 558–559](https://webspace.ship.edu/pgmarr/Geo441/Readings/Dixon%20and%20Mood%201946%20-%20The%20Statistical%20Sign%20Test.pdf); [NIST Sign Test](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/signtest.htm).

The mean intervals resample 128 *whole paired units* within a size stratum, retaining raw zero and tiny nonzero differences, then take the empirical 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles from 10,000 mean replicates. Ties/discreteness can put appreciable mass at the same bootstrap mean or endpoints; 10,000 draws reduce Monte Carlo error but do not create independent cases or guarantee 95% finite-sample coverage. These intervals are marginal/descriptive, not simultaneous 24-comparison intervals and not inversions of the Holm-adjusted sign tests. A zero-excluding mean interval and a non-rejected sign test can coexist because their estimands and multiplicity treatment differ. The last three sentences are inferences from the declared procedure and source methods, not claims that Efron proved coverage for this generator. [Efron 1979, Section 2](https://blogs.helsinki.fi/bk-club/files/2012/05/Efron_Bootstrap_AS1979.pdf); [Efron 1987, Section 3](https://www.scribd.com/document/887848463/Journal-of-the-American-Statistical-Association-1987-Mar-Vol-82-Iss-397-Efron-Bradley-Better-Bootstrap-Confidence-Intervals-1987-10-1080-016).

Holm's familywise claim concerns the 24 predeclared sign-test nulls if their component p values are valid under the paired-unit sampling assumptions. It does not adjust the bootstrap intervals or justify optional stopping. Reused paired cases induce dependence across comparisons; the step-down Bonferroni argument does not require those tests to be mutually independent, although the validity of each sign test still needs its own assumptions. [Holm, Section 2 and Theorem 1](https://www.scribd.com/document/475646736/Holm1979).
